# German Credit — Validation Report

Generated by `python -m scripts.benchmark` from actual execution.

## Provenance and protocol

[UCI Statlog German Credit, ID 144](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/144/statlog+german+credit+data). Hofmann (1994), DOI: https://doi.org/10.24432/C5NC77; CC BY 4.0. See `../data/README.md` for attribution and raw-file provenance.

Input: 1000 rows, 20 features. SHA-256: `b21f3d81db8071257d5ff1deaeba1fd4303b62712e6fcc9715c7a86202cb5871`.

Positive class: bad credit = 1 (original UCI 2); good credit = 0 (original UCI 1).

Seed 42; stratified 80/20 split; 800 training and 200 holdout rows. Training-only 5-fold shuffled StratifiedKFold; identical folds for both models.

Train counts (0/1): {0: 560, 1: 240}; holdout counts (0/1): {0: 140, 1: 60}.

LR: C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=2000. RF: 200 trees, unlimited depth. Other parameters use the pinned sklearn defaults. No hyperparameter search or holdout-driven selection.

Numerical median imputation; LR-only standardization. Categorical most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding with unseen categories ignored. All learned preprocessing is fitted inside each training fold. Final fits use training rows only.

Decision rule: P(bad) >= 0.5. Precision, recall and F1 treat bad as positive; ROC-AUC uses P(bad). Undefined precision/F1 are set to zero.

## Errors, costs and possible overfitting

Confusion matrices use actual rows and predicted columns, both ordered [good, bad]: `[[TN, FP], [FN, TP]]`.

UCI benchmark cost = FP + 5×FN; correct predictions cost zero. Mean cost = total / rows. These are relative benchmark units, not dollars or real-world bank losses. The 0.5 threshold is a fixed classification baseline, not a cost-optimal policy. A theoretical 1/6 cost threshold assumes calibrated probabilities and applicable costs; neither is established here. No threshold was selected on holdout data.

### Logistic Regression

Holdout matrix: `[[124, 16], [28, 32]]`. False negatives: 28; false positives: 16. Cost contributions: missed bad risks 140, good risks flagged bad 16.

At the fixed threshold, Logistic Regression and Random Forest have higher holdout benchmark cost than always predicting bad. This is a failure of these fixed decision rules under the supplied costs, not a recommendation to reject every applicant. Accuracy alone misses this tradeoff.

## Limitations and next validation steps

- Small historical German credit sample; donated in 1994, with legacy Deutsche Mark categories. Modern Canadian credit decisions and populations may differ.
- Random splitting cannot validate future-time or new-institution performance. Row separation does not prove person-level independence; no reliable grouping/time metadata is used here.
- Personal status/sex, age and foreign-worker fields raise fairness and proxy concerns. No subgroup fairness, legal compliance, probability calibration or drift evaluation was completed; removing a field alone would not establish fairness.
- No missing values in this official snapshot; synthetic missing-value tests only verify pipeline mechanics. They do not demonstrate robustness to real missingness.
- Aggregate errors identify missed bad risks and good risks flagged bad. They do not explain individual causes. Predictions and row IDs are saved for follow-up analysis.
- CV spread and the holdout provide limited evidence from one dataset and one fixed split. Future tuning must stay inside training data; repeated decisions based on this holdout would require a new independent final evaluation.
- Educational classical tabular benchmark only: no clinical or production suitability claim, and not validation of deep learning foundation models.
